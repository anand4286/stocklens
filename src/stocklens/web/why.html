Every stock site shows you numbers. StockLens tells you which ones are true.

Data aggregators are excellent at what they do: financial statements, ratios, analyst targets, charts. What none of them do is check. An analyst target is printed and never scored. An RSI is drawn and never tested on the stock it is drawn on. A beta is one number with no error bar. StockLens is built around the opposite question — would this have worked, and how sure can we be?

Principle 1 — noise is the null hypothesis

Every "is this real?" test is run against a seeded random walk in the test suite. The analog search fires on 5% of random walks — its nominal rate. The regime detector calls 100 of 100 random walks "random". A tool that finds edges in noise is broken, and this one is checked for it on every commit.

Principle 2 — missing is not neutral

A family with no data abstains; it lowers confidence instead of scoring zero. A Piotroski test that cannot be computed is "unknown", not "fail". A GARCH fit with fewer than 250 returns is withheld. A dash on this site means "we could not tell", and it is the honest answer more often than other sites admit.

Principle 3 — every number carries its rule

"Earnings yield 7.3% against a 5% line", never "undervalued". The bull and bear case is derived from the measurements, so it cannot contain a claim the page does not support, and a short bear case for clean accounts is the correct output rather than a gap to pad.

Principle 4 — the model has to earn its weights

The published weights are labelled as picked, not fitted. The regime tilt is labelled as a prior. The evaluation harness and the forecast ledger are the machinery that would earn or refute them — and if they refute them, the design records say what changes.

StockLens is not a data terminal and does not try to out-cover one. It is a research engine that shows its working, tests its own claims, and keeps score.
